# What Internet Measurement Data Is For

*An AI assisted, annotated inventory of reuse in M-Lab's public record of blog posts, 2013–2026*

A speed test answers a narrow question: how fast can this connection move data right now? The record it leaves behind can answer other questions. It includes throughput, latency, loss, the addresses at both ends, the path between them, and the conditions under which the test ran. Aggregated over millions of tests and fifteen years, that record becomes evidence about peering, disasters, pandemic demand, school connectivity, protocol design, regulation, and the measurement system itself.

This inventory collects examples of that reuse. It is not a list of everything M-Lab has published. It is a set of cases that show where the value came from: secondary use, joins with other data, reprocessing old raw data under better annotations, and public access that lets someone else ask a question no one planned for. The pattern is consistent. The value is rarely in the original test.

## Interconnection

The first large use of the record was interconnection. A long-running collaborative study used years of NDT measurements to compare access ISPs and transit providers. Consumers of AT&T, Comcast, CenturyLink, Time Warner Cable, and Verizon saw degraded throughput, higher latency, and more loss across particular access/transit pairs involving Cogent, Level 3, and XO. The degradation was concentrated in peak hours and appeared in multiple cities at the same time ([ISP Interconnection and its Impact on Consumer Internet Performance](https://measurementlab.net/blog/2014_interconnection_report/)).

The follow-up work made the method plain. Compare an access ISP's performance across multiple transit paths in the same metro, at the same time of day, and look for a persistent gap ([New Opportunities for Test Deployment and Continued Analysis of Interconnection Performance](https://measurementlab.net/blog/interconnection_and_measurement_update/)). AT&T customers over GTT in Chicago and Atlanta often saw peak-hour throughput below 0.1 Mbps while Comcast customers to the same sites did not. CenturyLink over Tata in Seattle fell below 1 Mbps while Cogent paths were healthy.

The most useful finding came from raw packet traces, not summary statistics. A sharp recovery across all affected access ISPs in February 2014 coincided with a change in the IP Type-of-Service marking on incoming packets: roughly a tenth of sampled measurements in early February, 30% by February 20, and about 94% five days later. Throughput rose at the same time ([Research Updates: Beginning to Observe Network Management Practices as a Third Party](https://measurementlab.net/blog/research_update1/)). The data could not establish who changed the marking or why. It did show that a performance time series could raise a specific question about network management.

A later reanalysis found that detectable congestion episodes declined after the 2015 Open Internet Order. Published interconnection agreements between Verizon and Level 3, and AT&T and GTT, were followed within days by measurable restoration ([Monitoring Interconnection Performance Since the Open Internet Order](https://measurementlab.net/blog/interconnection_update/)). The causal caution still applies. Measurement can establish association and timing. It cannot establish intent or responsibility. That is enough to make a market observable.

## Resilience

Most Internet failures are not outages. A network can stay reachable while latency rises or throughput collapses. HERMES was built to find those events in ordinary speed tests. It groups tests by access network, metro, and destination, compares each group with its recent baseline, and uses forward and reverse paths to localize the shared segment ([The Internet Can Be Up and Still Fail: Introducing HERMES](https://measurementlab.net/blog/hermes/)).

On 4 July 2024, Cogent users in Chicago testing to a Tata-hosted M-Lab server saw round-trip latency rise by more than 210 ms. The server-to-user path detoured through Texas, which explained about 30 ms. Reverse traceroutes showed the return path going through Singapore, which explained most of the rest. The usual Chicago–New York interconnection was absent. The public observatories we checked did not report the event. A Reddit thread with more than 100 comments did.

At scale, HERMES analyzed about 4 billion tests over five months and found roughly 65,000 performance events across 16,000 cities, 9,710 networks, and 166 countries. A denser cloud monitoring reimplementation detected 91.4% of the same events and agreed on the implicated segment 94.5% of the time. HERMES found 25 of 27 documented provider incidents, and about ten times as many publicly discussed problems as the public observatories in the same comparison. Half of the implicated links could only be identified by looking in both directions.

The same method turned a flood into a resilience map. During the September 2024 Andhra Pradesh flooding, the share of monitored networks with anomalies rose sharply near the flooded region and spread into neighboring areas, consistent with traffic rerouting onto shared fallback paths. Earlier, a different event produced a similar lesson. After Typhoon Haiyan, RIPE NCC researchers used open measurement data to conclude that damage to the Philippines' core infrastructure was moderate and localized ([Assessing Typhoon Haiyan's impact on core Internet infrastructure](https://measurementlab.net/blog/assessing_haiyans_impact/)).

## Demand shocks

COVID-19 created a demand shock no one designed. Within three weeks in March 2020, M-Lab's global NDT test rate rose from about 3,000 to over 7,000 tests per minute. In New York, interactive browser tests tripled while automated tests stayed flat, and median interactive download throughput fell 5–10 Mbps. In São Paulo, peak-hour medians fell from about 4 Mbps to 2 Mbps ([Changes Related to COVID-19](https://measurementlab.net/blog/covid19-test-rates-increase/)).

The automated cohort acted as a stable comparison. Its volume and client population did not change, and its median performance stayed flat while interactive performance fell. Publishing the dashboards alongside the analysis made the same data usable by people who do not write SQL ([How Measurement Lab's Open Data can be used during COVID-19](https://measurementlab.net/blog/datastudio-covid19-test-rates-increase/), [Accessible M-Lab NDT data for Internet Performance research during COVID-19](https://measurementlab.net/blog/covid-19-response-dashboards/)).

Northern Italy gave a cleaner before/during/after account. OONI and M-Lab found more than double the pre-pandemic volume of NDT tests after the lockdown began, reduced download and upload speeds through March–May 2020, and recovery to pre-lockdown levels as restrictions lifted ([Measurement Observations on Network Performance During the COVID-19 Pandemic in Northern Italy](https://measurementlab.net/blog/measurement-observations-on-network-performance-during-the-COVID-19-pandemic-in-Northern-Italy/)). The authors were explicit that the data shows correlation, that single tests cannot characterize a network, and that user-initiated tests carry self-selection bias. Those caveats are part of the method, not a weakness of the dataset.

## Access and policy

Measurement data changes decisions when it reaches a budget. Louisville integrated NDT into a city speed-test site and used the aggregated results to direct part of its fiber buildout funding toward underserved areas ([Local Communities are Driving Broadband Speed Mapping in the US](https://measurementlab.net/blog/communities-driving-broadband-mapping/)). Michigan's Moonshot pilot, built with Merit and Michigan State's Quello Center, sent students home with a measurement assignment to document the homework gap. More than 360,000 rural Michigan homes and 27% of K–12 students lacked home broadband ([Michigan Moonshot Data Collection Pilot Project Launches](https://measurementlab.net/blog/michigan-moonshot/)).

The same pattern works at the scale of a single institution. Murakami devices in U.S. public libraries produced repeated, location-known measurements rather than anonymous crowdsourced ones ([M-Lab's Murakami Tool](https://measurementlab.net/blog/murakami/)). A measurement computer installed with the Nation of Hawai'i was used to confirm that the community received the service it was promised ([Supporting Internet Self Determination at the 2019 Indigenous Connectivity Summit](https://measurementlab.net/blog/supporting-internet-self-determination-at-the-2019-indigenous-connectivity-summit/)).

The federal layer shows both the power and the trap. NTIA's Indicators of Broadband Need added M-Lab county medians to a map that already contained FCC Form 477 advertised speeds, Ookla measurements, and Microsoft application-level telemetry. These are four different instruments measuring four different things under one visual vocabulary ([NDT Data in NTIA Indicators of Broadband Need](https://measurementlab.net/blog/ntia/)).

Louisville and Michigan are the payoff. Baltimore is the caution. There, city-wide NDT data collapsed to only 50 distinct latitude/longitude points, and the author's own three home addresses geolocated outside the neighborhood. Community-area medians looked nearly identical because the underlying points did not resolve to communities ([Exploring NDT Data by Geography in Baltimore City](https://measurementlab.net/blog/exploring-geographic-limits-of-ip-geolocation/)). Geographic precision is a property of the collection design, not a property of the dataset. Local collection, premise devices, and volunteered location recover what IP annotation cannot.

The aggregation problem is just as concrete. In one Maryland postal code, an all-provider weighted average concealed Comcast users pulling the number up and two mobile, two satellite, one cable, and one business ISP pulling it down. The all-provider NDT average was 72.9/9.7 Mbps. The Ookla average for the same place was 293/11.65 Mbps ([ARC of Research pt. 2: Exploring Data Sources Relevant to Our Questions](https://measurementlab.net/blog/arc-research-pt2/)). The earlier installment argued that the question determines the instrument: on-net multi-stream tests for advertised access-link speeds, off-net single-stream tests for the path beyond the last mile ([ARC of Research pt. 1: Asking the Right Questions](https://measurementlab.net/blog/arc-of-research-pt1/)).

The policy recommendations follow from this. Aggregate by ISP, separate peak and off-peak, use histograms and log scales, and correct for client bias and population drift ([Using M-Lab Data in Broadband Advocacy and Policy](https://measurementlab.net/blog/mlab-data-policy-advocacy/)). A proposal that promises conclusions without these disciplines will produce confident, wrong maps.

## Consumer protection

Wehe turns a user's device into a differential-treatment detector. It replays captured traffic from a real application, then replays the same traffic with inverted content that no classifier should recognize, and compares the results ([Wehe Measurement Service Launched on M-Lab Platform](https://measurementlab.net/blog/wehe-launch/)). France's Arcep adopted it as an official tool. By the time of the launch post, close to 145,000 tests had been run in France with no differentiation detected.

Publishing Wehe data in BigQuery turned a consumer app into a longitudinal regulatory dataset. Researchers can ask which ISPs differentiate, which applications are affected, how throttling is implemented, and whether a practice tracks overload or persists all day across a country ([Wehe data is now available in BigQuery](https://measurementlab.net/blog/wehe-bigquery-announcement/)). Cloudflare's AIM data added a complementary end-user vocabulary—bandwidth, jitter, responsiveness—and published every test's score rather than only a summary ([M-Lab is now providing Cloudflare AIM data for open access](https://measurementlab.net/blog/cloudflare-aimscoredata-announcement/)).

The 2022 fellowships pushed the work from detection toward explanation. One project correlated NDT anomalies with BGP control-plane events. Another extended Wehe to localize where differentiation occurs. A third studied how to infer quality of experience from active and passive measurements ([M-Lab Research Fellows - Spring 2022](https://measurementlab.net/blog/research-fellow-announcement/)).

## Routing

Traceroutes record the path a connection takes. M-Lab generates about 14 million traceroutes per day and holds more than 8 billion `scamper1` rows covering roughly 480 million unique destination addresses. About 40% never reach the destination. Only 2.1–3.5 million per day are non-duplicate, with just 2.1 million of those complete ([An Overview of M-Lab's Traceroute Data](https://measurementlab.net/blog/overview-of-mlab-traceroute/)).

That is enough to study routing at Internet scale. Researchers have used it to show that African routing often leaves the continent and returns, inflating latency ([AFRICOMM 2018 and AFRINIC-29](https://measurementlab.net/blog/africomm-afrinic/)). It recorded the October 2021 Facebook outage as a spike. It shows that forward and reverse paths can take different routes because each network makes its own forwarding decisions.

Reverse traceroute closes the loop. Only about a quarter of NDT tests have a reverse path, but for those the two directions can diverge sharply. In HERMES's evaluation, the user-to-server route was geographically longer than the server-to-user route in 72% of anomalous cases and at least twice as long in 10% ([A Hands-On Tutorial with Reverse Traceroute](https://measurementlab.net/blog/revtr_tutorial/)). The IP Route Survey adds independent, continuous coverage of all routable IPv4 prefixes from ten vantage points, in a schema deliberately compatible with `scamper1`. A local anomaly can then be checked against the broader routing picture ([IP Route Survey data published in M-Lab](https://measurementlab.net/blog/iprs-data-on-mlab/)).

The barrier to entry is low. At hackathons, this stack has been pointed at the Russia–Ukraine conflict, IPv4/IPv6 differences, Hurricane Ian, and continental performance disparities in 90-minute sprints ([M-Lab Hackathon at IMC 2022](https://measurementlab.net/blog/imc22-hackathon/)). The binding constraint is questions, not access.

## Protocols and platform

NDT's history is also a history of TCP. NDT moved from Reno to Cubic to BBR, from web100 to `tcp-info`, and from raw sockets to WebSockets on standard ports. Each change altered what the instrument measures, and each is documented so that longitudinal comparisons can be made rather than assumed ([Evolution of NDT](https://measurementlab.net/blog/evolution-of-ndt/)).

BBR matters because model-based congestion control can fill high-latency, high-loss paths that loss-based TCP cannot. That changes both user experience and the meaning of a speed result ([BBR TCP and Measurement Lab](https://measurementlab.net/blog/tcp-and-bbr/)). Neubot data from 2012–2018 across more than 90 countries and up to 4,500 volunteer computers showed the opposite problem. The upper end of transfer speeds grew much faster than the lower end, leaving a growing underload regime in which modern congestion control gets too little feedback to use the capacity that exists ([Guest Post: Underload: Today's Problem of Internet Congestion Control](https://measurementlab.net/blog/underload/)).

The instrument's own costs have become a research subject. At 1 Gbps, a 10-second NDT test moves 1.25 GB. Capping the Google Search integration at 250 MB saves about 25% of transferred data with a 95th-percentile throughput error of about 5%, while leaving 91% of clients unaffected because they never reach the cap ([Introducing Data Transfer Limits to NDT](https://measurementlab.net/blog/short-ndt/)). MSAK added multi-stream throughput and UDP latency, jitter, and loss, partly to satisfy the FCC's three-stream mobile measurement requirement and partly to make bufferbloat and real-time quality observable ([Introducing MSAK](https://measurementlab.net/blog/introducing-msak/)).

Cloud deployment expanded the path diversity. M-Lab ran a virtual site in all 35 Google Cloud regions, with 41 VMs and about 13% of traffic at announcement. Cloud nodes showed lower loss than physical sites in Mumbai and behaved correctly under a test spike ([M-Lab to the Cloud: Virtual sites in all Google Cloud regions](https://measurementlab.net/blog/virtual-sites-gcp/), [M-Lab to the Cloud](https://measurementlab.net/blog/2022-mlab-to-the-cloud/)). By the end of 2023, more than 30% of traffic measured to cloud servers ([2023 Year in Review](https://measurementlab.net/blog/end-of-year-letter-2023/)).

## The instrument as evidence

Some of the most useful secondary findings are about the measurement system, not the Internet. A 2015 duplication bug doubled every test for a given day at a given site. Because the study relied on daily medians, the published findings were unchanged, while monthly medians shifted by at most about 10% ([Data Duplication Bug in M-Lab BigQuery Data](https://measurementlab.net/blog/data_duplication_writeup/)).

A Paris Traceroute race condition silently cut coverage from 85% of NDT tests to about 5%. Because the failures were geographically systematic, the missing data was not missing at random ([Paris Traceroute Brownout](https://measurementlab.net/blog/paris-traceroute-brownout/)). A separate tag-collision bug contaminated 2.7% of traceroutes since July 2016 by mixing hops from concurrent paths. The fix included detection, reprocessing, and preserving the raw archive untouched ([Paris Traceroute has a bug, and it causes some bad data](https://measurementlab.net/blog/pt-bug/)).

Operational telemetry became a dataset in its own right. DISCO collects 10-second switch counters—unicast, broadcast, multicast, discards, errors, pause frames, octets—for every machine port and site uplink, originally to diagnose microburst discards ([New DISCO switch telemetry dataset](https://measurementlab.net/blog/disco-dataset/)). When 10 Gbps servers on 1 Gbps uplinks showed increased discards in 2018, the team used that telemetry plus NDT and SideStream data to show the effect was below the noise floor of Internet performance, and published the notebook ([Switch discard investigation for early 2018](https://measurementlab.net/blog/switch-discards-redux/)). The original microburst incident had already produced a durable `blacklist_flags` field so users could exclude affected tests ([Traffic Microbursts and their Effect on Internet Measurement](https://measurementlab.net/blog/traffic-microbursts-and-their-effect-on-internet-measurement/)).

Annotations have their own history. Geographic fields moved from MaxMind GeoLite Legacy with FIPS-10-4 regions to GeoLite2 with ISO 3166-2 subdivisions. ASN annotations moved to CAIDA RouteViews so that an IP maps to the network that owned it at test time ([Evolution of M-Lab's Geographic and Network Annotations](https://measurementlab.net/blog/evolution-of-annotations/)). The transition broke queries that filtered on `client.Geo.Region` for two years of ndt7 data. That is a concrete example of why schema stability and provenance are part of the data's value ([Updates to Geo Filters](https://measurementlab.net/blog/updates-to-geo-filters/)).

Geolocation has two systems: App Engine metadata for server selection, and MaxMind for publication. Comparing them reveals inconsistencies. Filtering to consistent locations removes 18.4% of IPs in Africa but only 7.9% in South America, which is both a quality control and a warning about coverage bias ([How M-Lab Determines User Location and Selects Servers](https://measurementlab.net/blog/improving-m-lab-geolocation/)). Load management is measurement science too. Non-randomized automated clients created synchronized bursts that degraded their own and others' tests, which is why ndt7 began requiring Locate-issued access tokens ([Requiring access tokens for ndt7](https://measurementlab.net/blog/why-access-tokens/)).

## From speed to quality

The largest change in the last few years is the move from speed to quality. The Internet Quality Barometer convened more than 60 experts across research, policy, advocacy, and industry to build a composite score across use cases—browsing, video, gaming, conferencing—each with its own latency, throughput, and loss requirements ([Measurement Lab publishes the Internet Quality Barometer Framework](https://measurementlab.net/blog/iqb/)).

The framework is deliberately auditable. An open-source library and a sensitivity analysis exist. A current fellowship is testing whether scores remain stable across threshold choices, use-case weights, data sparsity, and urban–rural gaps, and whether countries with fragmented markets are systematically misrepresented ([IQB Meets Reliability: New Research on M-Lab's Internet Quality Barometer](https://measurementlab.net/blog/pulse-fellow-iqb/)). That is the right kind of secondary question: not only whether the index works, but for whom it fails and how we would know.

The school-connectivity adaptation shows how far the record can travel. Giga, a UNICEF–ITU initiative, runs Giga Meter in schools. The IQB-Edu prototype uses 66 urban and 15 rural Moldovan schools, four measurements per day, with 95th-percentile performance as a capacity proxy and median as a typical-experience proxy ([Connectivity CoP — May Session Recap](https://measurementlab.net/blog/cop-may-meeting/)). The QoE design document extends NDT's bulk-transfer regime with DNS lookups over UDP and HTTPS, timed fetches of education-relevant web resources across DNS/TCP/TLS stages, fixed-rate TCP streaming, and MSAK multi-stream tests ([RFC: Design Document on Quality of Experience Measurements for Schools](https://measurementlab.net/blog/cop-qoe-design-document/)).

The Community of Practice around this work has four workstreams: better tools, facility characterization, IQB-Edu, and new insights from existing data. Its membership spans universities, public agencies, and rural network operators ([Connectivity Community of Practice Launch](https://measurementlab.net/blog/cop-launch/), [M-Lab and Giga to Launch Community of Practice](https://measurementlab.net/blog/giga-mlab-cop/)). The Vermont drive-test program, UNHCR's displacement-context work, and the IQB-Edu policy notebooks sit in the same conversation ([Connectivity CoP — July Session Recap](https://measurementlab.net/blog/cop-july-meeting/)).

## The infrastructure of reuse

Reuse does not happen because data is public. It happens when the cost of use is low and the methods are shared. BigQuery tables turned some two-hour queries into eight-second queries ([Making it Easier to Use M-Lab Data](https://measurementlab.net/blog/new-tables-release/)). RIPEstat widgets let anyone visualize bandwidth distribution and active-address distribution by country or network ([Explore M-Lab data using the new RIPEstat widgets](https://measurementlab.net/blog/ripestat_mlab_widgets/)). The observatory and visualization site made ISP comparisons legible without SQL ([New Features for M-Lab Internet Observatory](https://measurementlab.net/blog/observatory-new-features/)).

The current Monthly Stats dataset is 18 GB of precomputed summaries from 2009–2026 by country, subdivision, and ASN, with example notebooks in preparation ([Connectivity CoP — July Session Recap](https://measurementlab.net/blog/cop-july-meeting/)). An autoloading pipeline publishes ten new data types—Wehe, reverse traceroute, MSAK, and host telemetry among them—and Cloudflare's speed tests through the same ingestion path ([2023 Year in Review](https://measurementlab.net/blog/end-of-year-letter-2023/)). The dynamic fleet adds ndt7 views with provenance for servers outside the legacy fleet, so growth in vantage points does not silently break longitudinal analysis ([Measurement Lab publishes new dataset of NDT7 measurements](https://measurementlab.net/blog/dynamic-data-bq/)).

The human layer matters as much as the pipeline. Fellowships turn open questions into funded work. Hackathons produce reproducible Colab notebooks in 90 minutes. The Open Measurement Gatherings built trust and a shared user guide across M-Lab, OONI, IODA, and Censored Planet ([Open Measurement Gathering](https://measurementlab.net/blog/open-measurement-gathering-1/), [OMG User Guide and fourth convening](https://measurementlab.net/blog/omg4/), [OMG AMA](https://measurementlab.net/blog/omg3/)).

M-Lab also publishes the cases where openness has costs. The SamKnows experiment left the platform when it would not provide an open-source server, despite years of partnership with the FCC's Measuring Broadband America program ([Recent updates regarding the SamKnows experiment](https://measurementlab.net/blog/mlab-public-statement-samknows-experiment/)). Openness is enforced technically, not just declared.
